Check-payer conversion

LIVE PAYMENTS CONVERSION FINTECH
View live prototype GitHub repo

What it is

An interactive, clickable prototype and stakeholder walkthrough for improving conversion in an HOA payments platform's automated check-payer conversion campaign — an email nudge designed to move homeowners off paper checks and onto digital payment.

The problem

The campaign's beta was converting only 11% of targeted check payers, with the largest leak at the very top of the funnel: 66% of recipients never opened the email at all. Downstream, guest checkout treated every homeowner identically regardless of whether they'd ever paid digitally before — asking people who already had a saved payment method to re-enter it from scratch. With the campaign about to move to General Availability across every managed association, and only 1–2 weeks of engineering time available, the task was to pick one high-leverage intervention and prove it out.

The solution

A messaging variant built around a paperless/environmental angle rather than generic "convenience" copy — chosen specifically to lift open rate, the stage with the biggest drop-off, and validated by prior experience running payment-adoption campaigns at Entrata. The design went a step further within that same intervention: the email's call-to-action adapts based on a signal the platform already has — whether the homeowner has a saved payment method. Homeowners without one land in a guided guest checkout; homeowners with one get a scoped magic-link straight to a pre-filled payment screen, skipping the friction entirely. A post-payment "set up autopay in one click" prompt was included as a fast, low-cost bonus addressing a separate retention signal (only 68% of first-time digital converters repeated their payment method next cycle) — explicitly scoped as outside the graded intervention.

Why it matters

This reframes the funnel's biggest lever as the inbox, not the checkout form — a testable, low-engineering-cost hypothesis that directly targets the largest quantified drop-off point in the data, rather than spreading limited engineering time across multiple unproven ideas. The segmented CTA approach also demonstrates that "pick one enhancement" doesn't mean "ignore known user state" — it means building that one enhancement well enough to account for the real population it's serving.

How it was built

Designed and built as a fully clickable prototype using AI-assisted rapid development (Claude) — phone-frame mockups simulating the actual inbox-to-checkout-to-confirmation flow, with realistic autofill interactions, PCI-accurate payment handling (no stored CVV), and a live progress indicator. Delivered as a single self-contained HTML/CSS/JS file, published via GitHub Pages for stakeholder click-through access, and paired with a scripted 5-minute Loom walkthrough covering the behavioral insight, live demo, testing plan (funnel analysis, A/B testing), and iteration path.

Skills demonstrated

Data-driven prioritization under tight engineering constraints; behavioral/conversion-psychology-informed product design; payments domain expertise (PCI-DSS, ACH vs. card economics, deep-link auth scoping); rapid AI-assisted prototyping and technical fluency; structuring and communicating a testing/rollout plan (experiment design, success metrics, guardrails); and concise, stakeholder-ready storytelling under a hard time constraint.

KPI Framework

How I'd Measure Success

This concept was never deployed, so there's no live before/after data. Here's the segmented testing framework I'd use to validate it.

Email open rate

Whether the paperless messaging angle actually lifts opens — the single largest drop-off point in the original funnel — tracked overall and by segment.

Click-through rate

Whether an opened email converts to a click into the flow, tracked by segment (A: no saved payment method, B: saved payment method) to separate a messaging effect from a routing effect.

Payment-setup completion — Segment A

Completion rate for homeowners without a saved method, moving through guided guest checkout. Nothing about their checkout mechanics changed in this test, so this isolates whether messaging alone moved completion — expected roughly flat.

Payment completion — Segment B

Completion rate for homeowners with a saved method, routed straight to a pre-filled screen via magic link. This is where the friction-removal effect should show up as a real lift.

Unsubscribe / opt-out rate

Guardrail metric — a punchier, more urgent-feeling subject line can lift opens while quietly increasing complaints or opt-outs. Tracked to confirm the win isn't coming at the cost of trust.

Post-payment autopay setup rate

Tracked in the prototype as a bonus signal, but explicitly out of scope for grading this intervention — a separate lever addressing repeat-payment retention, not the check-to-digital conversion this case study is scoped to.
